# How teams are created and named

Teams live inside an organisation. A team is what gets entered into competitions and what plays in matches. Names are built for you from a few structured choices, so every team reads consistently across standings, matches and stats.

## Where a team's name comes from

You don't type a team name. You choose its parts, and MatchPulse composes the name from them. Every team is one of two shapes:

- A **senior side** — an ordinal from **1st Team** to **10th Team**.
- An **age side** — an **age group** (U19 down to U8) with a **letter** (A to J), e.g. **U16A**.

That choice is the same for schools, clubs and associations.

For a school called Riverside College, you might have:

- Riverside College 1st Team
- Riverside College 2nd Team
- Riverside College U16A

The school is the organisation. Each side is a separate team within it.

## Gender and division

- **Schools** take their gender from the school itself. A boys-only or girls-only school applies its gender automatically. A co-ed school chooses Boys or Girls for each team — which is how a co-ed school can hold both **Boys U14A** and **Girls U14A**.
- **Clubs and associations** choose a **division** (Men's, Ladies, Masters, Open, Boys, Girls) that sits on top of the same level selector, e.g. **Men's 1st Team**.

A school must have its gender profile set before you can add teams — it is never guessed.

## Why it's structured

Because the name is built from fields rather than typed, teams sort correctly by seniority, duplicates are caught automatically, and "U16A" always means the same thing everywhere it appears. There is no free-text team name to keep consistent — the format is consistent by construction.

## How naming plays out in competitions

When a team is entered into a competition, its composed name carries through to the match list and the standings table, led by the organisation name. You don't repeat the season or competition in the team — that context comes from the competition itself.
